# Su Hui's Star Gauge (璇璣圖)

An interactive guide to the *Xuanji Tu*, the reversible poem attributed to the fourth-century poet Su Hui: 841 characters in a 29 × 29 square that can be read as thousands of rhymed poems.

## Viewing it

Open `index.html` in a browser.

To share it as a web page, switch on GitHub Pages for this repository (Settings, then Pages, then "Deploy from a branch" with the `main` branch and the `/ (root)` folder). The page is then served at `https://<your-username>.github.io/<repository-name>/`.

## What the page does

- Point at any character for its pinyin, an English gloss, its position and the section it belongs to.
- A labelled plan of the frame, corners, sides and centre, with the line length each one is read in.
- Sixteen readings traced across the grid, each with pinyin, a word-by-word gloss and a translation.
- "Walk the frame": compose seven-character quatrains by choosing a direction at each crossing.
- Notes on what makes the poem remarkable, and a history from the fourth century to 2020.

## About the text and translations

- The Chinese text follows the transcription in *Flowers in the Mirror* (鏡花緣) as given on Chinese Wikisource, with two corrections. Circulating editions differ in about twenty places, and the page flags those characters.
- The colour scheme follows *Flowers in the Mirror*. The original colouring of the brocade is lost.
- Pinyin is modern Mandarin, not the pronunciation of Su Hui's time.
- The glosses and translations were drafted with Claude, an AI model. They are not taken from a published translation and have not been reviewed by a specialist. For published versions see David Hinton, *Classical Chinese Poetry: An Anthology* (2008), and Michèle Métail, *Wild Geese Returning: Chinese Reversible Poems* (2017).
- Su Hui's poem itself is in the public domain.

## Files

- `index.html`: the whole page (markup, styles, script and data) in one file. There is no build step. The typefaces load from Google Fonts.
